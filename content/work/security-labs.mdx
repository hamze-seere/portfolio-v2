## Overview

These labs work through security fundamentals using the university itself as the case: its student records, email, registrar laptops, web portal and campus network. Each lab is documented in its own repository.

## Risk and incident analysis

- **Lab 1 — Attack surface.** An inventory of ten university assets, each mapped to a realistic threat, a risk rating and a control — from SQL injection on the student records database (WAF + input validation) to rogue access points on campus Wi-Fi (WPA3 + NAC monitoring).
- **Lab 2 — Incident impact mapping.** A ransomware scenario at the Registrar's Office: a phishing attachment (`Invoice.pdf.exe`) infects 15 machines in 30 minutes. I mapped the immediate consequences — locked machines, encrypted records, email down, registration halted — and the wider impact.
- **Lab 3 — CIA classification.** Fifteen scenarios classified by confidentiality, integrity or availability, each with a matching preventive, detective or corrective control.

## Systems and networks

- **Lab 4 — System inventory** and a hardening checklist for a lab VM.
- **Lab 5 — Windows/Linux security baseline**, starting from least-privilege lab accounts.
- **Lab 6 — A small network in Cisco Packet Tracer**: router, switch and end devices for staff and students.
- **Lab 7 — IP configuration and connectivity** testing and documentation.
- **Lab 8 — Service identification** on an isolated VM: find what is running, then reduce it to what is approved.

All hands-on work was done in isolated, authorised lab environments.
